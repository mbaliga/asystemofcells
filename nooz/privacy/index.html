Privacy Policy

Last updated 26 July 2026

Nooz is a personal news reader. You choose the sources you follow, and Nooz shows you what flowed from them alongside what you actually read. This page explains what happens to your information. The guiding idea is simple: Nooz is yours, and so is your reading.

The short version

No account. No ads. No third-party analytics or tracking. Your sources, your reading history, and your settings live on your device, not on our servers. The only time information leaves your device is to fetch the news articles you asked to read.

What Nooz stores, and where

Everything Nooz remembers about you is kept locally on your device — in your browser's storage on the web, or in the app's private storage on Android:

  • the sources, regions, and topics you follow;
  • which articles you have opened or marked as read, used to show your reading against what your sources published;
  • your clippings, filters, and reading preferences;
  • any AI provider key you choose to add (see below).

Nooz has no user accounts and no sign-in. This information is not sent to us, and we cannot see it. If you clear your browser storage or uninstall the app, it is gone.

Fetching the news

To display a feed or an article, Nooz has to request it from the publisher that hosts it. That request is used only to retrieve and show you the content you asked for — it is never used to build a profile of you.

In the web reader, those requests pass through a small proxy we host. This is a technical necessity, not a data grab: web browsers block a page from reading a feed on another website (a rule called CORS), so the proxy fetches the feed or article on your browser's behalf and hands back the content. The proxy does not store your reading activity or associate requests with you. As with any website, our hosting provider (Vercel) may generate ordinary, short-lived technical logs while serving requests; these are not used to track or identify you.

On Android, the app fetches feeds directly and no proxy of ours is involved.

Optional AI features

Nooz's on-device features — Nooz Cast (text-to-speech that reads articles aloud) and Nooz Flash (a compressed news digest) — run entirely on your device using models downloaded to it. The text they process does not leave your device.

Separately, on Android you may optionally bring your own key for a third-party AI provider. If you do, the key is stored only on your device, and requests go directly from your device to that provider (for example, OpenAI) under that provider's own terms and privacy policy. We do not receive your key or those requests.

Crash diagnostics

If the Android app crashes, it writes a single diagnostic report to the app's private storage on your device so you can see what went wrong. Nothing is transmitted — there is no crash-reporting network call and no third-party crash SDK. You can read, export, or delete that report yourself in Settings.

No advertising, no tracking

Nooz shows no ads, includes no advertising or analytics SDKs, and does not sell or share personal information. There is no cross-site tracking and no behavioural profiling.

Third-party content

The articles you read come from the publishers you choose to follow. Each of those sites has its own privacy practices, which apply when you open a link to read the full story on their site. Those practices are outside our control.

Children

Nooz is a general-audience news reader and is not directed at children under 13. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a meaningful way, we will update the date at the top of this page. Because Nooz keeps no accounts, we have no way to email you about changes, so the current version always lives here.

Contact

Nooz is made by A System of Cells. If you have a question about this policy or your information, you can reach us at nooz@asystemofcells.com.
